## 2 · Executive summary

1. **Seven slides ship with literal `IMAGE:` placeholder text in the render** — slides 12, 15, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21. These are gray boxes with italic art-direction notes visible to the audience. This is a P0 blocker. The dashed gray boxes alone are tolerable; the italicized prompt text inside them is not.
2. **Slide 11 contradicts itself on its own headline thesis.** The subhead says "Tech is growing 4× faster — at a *lower* multiple." The left bar chart shows Tech P/E 23× vs Non-Tech 27× (Tech lower, consistent with the subhead). The dark payoff band directly below shows "Tech median: ~26× ... Non-Tech: ~23×" (Tech *higher* — computed from the TOP20 table). Both cannot be true. The computed medians from the data are correct; the hardcoded `23 / 27` in the bar-chart `metrics` array and the "lower multiple" phrase in the subhead are wrong. This is the headline-breaking error of the deck.
3. **Title quality is uneven.** Strong, takeaway-style titles: slides 6, 9, 21 (and to a degree 3, 22). Everything else is a topic label with a period — "The labs.", "Earnings & valuations.", "Supply chain fragility.", "Physical AI." — which forces the subhead to do all the "so what" lifting. For an institutional roadshow deck, every content title should be a full-sentence conclusion.
4. **The appendix (slide A2) is truncating content.** The RISKS column ends around "EU AI Act Aug 2, 2026 enforcement" and the slide 17 citation block (NIMBY, anti-AI sentiment, labor gap) appears to be cut off in the render. A stray "ASML filings" fragment shows at the bottom of the column — looks like overflow, not intended content.
5. **Several hero claims need sourcing before the room asks.** "~48% hyperscaler net debt/EBITDA" (slide 14) is suspicious — the MSFT/GOOGL/AMZN/META/ORCL group is close to net-cash in aggregate. The xAI/SpaceX "$1.25T all-stock merger closed Feb 2, 2026" (slide 10) and Anthropic's implied ~$29B ARR (13× on $380B, slide 10) will get questioned. "~12% of US electricity by 2028" (slide 9) is the top of the EPRI range, not a point estimate. Call these out or dial them back.
6. **Chart-to-chart consistency breaks twice.** Slide 6 pairs an AI-vs-Internet-vs-*PC* adoption chart with an AI-vs-*Cloud*-vs-Internet revenue chart — the comparison set isn't the same, and the AI line on the left stops at Y3 but the X-axis extends to Y15, leaving a visually empty 12 years. Slide 11's scatter clips Tesla at 50× (true 190×) and clips NVDA/AVGO growth at +50% — these clips are annotated in the source but not on the chart itself.
7. **Text wrap bug on slide 8.** The stacked bar labels "SOVEREIGNS" (12% segment) and "ENTERPRISE" (10% segment) wrap as "SOVEREIGN / S" and "ENTERPR / ISE". The segments are too narrow for the labels.
8. **Systematic empty-space problem.** Slides 4, 5, 8, 9, 19 use card heights that were sized for denser copy; body text currently fills only the top 30–50% of each card, leaving large empty gray zones. Either tighten card heights or add data.

## 3 · Slide-by-slide review

### Slide 1 — AI Markets (cover)
**What works:** Bold, confident wordmark treatment. Yellow + orange + black reads as a deliberate brand system, not a default template. The orange accent rule anchors the mark nicely.
**What doesn't:** No firm/author identification anywhere on the cover — no "Strategy Research," no presenter name, no distribution tag. For a roadshow deck, the audience should know who it's from at the first glance. The footer disclaimer is rendering at 6.5pt with kerning so tight that letters look like they're splitting ("INDICA TIVE" / "PA ST PERFORMANCE") across every slide — not a content typo but a real visual artifact.
**Specific suggestions:**
- Add a small "Strategy Research" lockup or presenter line under "May 2026".
- Bump the disclaimer footer text from 6.5pt to 8pt and reduce `charSpacing` so kerning doesn't artifact.
- Consider a subtitle ("AI's capex cycle, repricing, and frontier" or similar) under the date so the cover says what the deck is about.

### Slide 2 — Contents.
**What works:** Color-coded section bars and the 01–06 numbering; aligns with the theme tags used on every content slide. Page ranges on the right are useful for navigation.
**What doesn't:** The title "Contents." is fine for a TOC. The source line — "Source: Strategy Research. 22 slides, read top to bottom." — is both unnecessary (a TOC doesn't need a source) and technically wrong (the deck has 22 content slides + 2 appendix = 24 total, but the footer page numbers run 2 through 22, with A1/A2 for appendix).
**Specific suggestions:**
- Delete the source line. Replace with a one-line positioning note: "Read as three arcs: the capex cycle, what's repricing inside it, and what comes next."
- Consider adding a small "APPENDIX · A1–A2" row below the six sections, in muted gray, so the appendix isn't a surprise.

### Slide 3 — Why AI matters to markets.
**What works:** Three big colored number blocks land the three arguments clearly. The "It is repricing everything — in both directions" third point is the strongest line in the deck because it sets up Slide 13's divergence.
**What doesn't:** "Big 5 hyperscalers are pouring roughly $750B into AI infrastructure in 2026 alone — more than the entire US energy buildout at its peak." The comparison is unfalsifiable and will get pushed back on. US energy capex (oil/gas/power/transmission combined) has run $300–500B/yr; "at its peak" is undefined. This sounds overclaimed.
**Specific suggestions:**
- Rewrite the point-01 body: replace "more than the entire US energy buildout at its peak" with something verifiable like "larger than global semiconductor revenue, and roughly 3× total US oil-and-gas capex." Cite.
- The title "Why AI matters to markets." is adequate but label-ish. Tighter: "AI is the largest capex cycle in history — and it's touching every layer of the market."

### Slide 4 — AI evolution from chatbots to agents.
**What works:** Timeline metaphor with dot + card per phase is a clean visual system. The two "hero" phases (LLM chatbots, Agentic AI) are tinted lighter so the eye lands on them — good hierarchy.
**What doesn't:**
- **Date wrong.** The "Reasoning" phase is labeled `2023–24`. o1 launched Sept 2024 and DeepSeek R1 Jan 2025 — so the phase is `2024–25`, not `2023–24`. As written, Reasoning ends before the named products exist.
- The phase cards are 3.6" tall but the body copy fills ~1" of that. The lower 2/3 of every card is empty gray.
- Labels overlap the dividing rule: "Machine Learning" wraps to two lines while other phases are one — uneven vertical rhythm.
**Specific suggestions:**
- Change `1990s–2000s / Machine Learning / Systems learn patterns from data, not rules.` date-range spacing, and change `2023–24 / Reasoning` to `2024–25 / Reasoning`.
- Shrink card height from 3.6 to ~2.2 so the cards aren't 70% whitespace, and close the gap to the footer.
- Consider adding a small "TODAY" tick above the Agentic AI column (between Reasoning and Agentic dots) to orient the viewer.

### Slide 5 — The AI stack.
**What works:** The "USER → COMPUTE → SILICON → MATERIALS" rail is genuinely useful — the deck uses this taxonomy again in the appendix, so it earns its real estate.
**What doesn't:**
- The title "The AI stack." is a pure label. The slide gets no subhead either, so there is literally no "so what" on this page.
- Grouping "Equipment" and "Raw materials" under "MATERIALS" is a category error. Semiconductor capital equipment (ASML, AMAT, Lam, KLA) is not a material — it's the tooling. Readers who know the space will flag this.
- Empty space between the headline rule and the first row is ~1" of whitespace. Slide feels underweight.
**Specific suggestions:**
- Add a subhead: "Closer to the user is where value accrues to software margins; closer to the atoms is where bottlenecks reprice — and both ends are AI-exposed."
- Rename the "MATERIALS" rail to "TOOLS + MATERIALS" or split into "EQUIPMENT" (Equipment) and "MATERIALS" (Raw materials).
- Rewrite the title: "Value concentrates at two ends of the AI stack — and the middle is the bottleneck."
- Add a small payoff band at the bottom calling out which 3 layers are absorbing the most capital today.

### Slide 6 — AI growth rates are unprecedented.
**What works:** Strong title (full-sentence takeaway). The AI curve visibly punches through Internet and PC trajectories — the visual does the work of the headline.
**What doesn't:**
- **Left chart has 12 empty years.** AI values are `[0, 18, 38, 56, null × 12]` but the chart shows Y0–Y15 on the x-axis. The AI line stops at Y3 with nothing projected. The empty half of the chart is visually noisy.

- **Yellow payoff banner repeats the subhead verbatim.** Subhead: "User adoption and revenue growth for AI far eclipse prior technologies — a once-in-a-generation S-curve, compressed into a handful of years." Banner: "User adoption and revenue growth for AI far eclipse prior technologies." Drop the banner or replace with a different point.
- "AI revenue Y0–Y4" — the source is "company filings and consensus estimates." Which company(ies)? OpenAI+Anthropic? Add a Y0 anchor date ("Y0 = 2022, ChatGPT's first full year") so the viewer can orient.
**Specific suggestions:**
- Truncate the left chart's x-axis to Y0–Y5 (or extend the AI line with an explicit dashed projection through Y5 or Y6 so the curve keeps the spotlight).
- Replace the right chart with AI/Cloud/PC or AI/Internet/Mobile — keep the comparison set consistent with the left.
- Rewrite the yellow banner: "Every prior tech cycle was an S-curve. AI is an S-curve compressed into 4 years — because the capex and the demand are arriving together."
- Add a Y0 note beneath each chart ("Y0 = ChatGPT consumer launch, Nov 2022").

### Slide 7 — Semiconductors, explained.
**What works:** The six product-category cards are clean, tight, and each has a plain-English definition + representative use + top players. This is the best teaching slide in the deck.
**What doesn't:**

- Title "Semiconductors, explained." is a weak label.
**Specific suggestions:**
- Rework the top strip so Equipment sits *above* Foundry with an arrow pointing *down into* it, not sideways from Design. Or drop the chevron between Design and Equipment entirely and add a small "supplies" tag instead.
- Rewrite the title: "Chips flow through four stages and six end-markets — AI concentrates in two of them."
- The yellow payoff band is strong — keep as is.

### Slide 9 — Power is the new bottleneck.
**What works:** One of the best slides. Strong takeaway title. Three crisp stats, three power-source cards. "GPUs are useless without electrons" is a memorable closer.
**What doesn't:**
- **"1 GW = mid-sized city" mismatch.** Subhead says "as much electricity as a mid-sized city." Stat card says "the draw of ~750K US homes." 750K homes is a major metro (roughly Atlanta, Seattle, or Minneapolis scale), not a mid-sized city.
- **"~12% by 2028" is the top of the EPRI range.** EPRI (2024) projected 4.6%–9.1% central / high range. A 12% figure is achievable under a specific high-growth scenario but not the central estimate. Own that explicitly.
- Card bodies leave ~0.3" of empty space under the players line.
**Specific suggestions:**
- Rewrite the subhead: "A single hyperscale AI campus now draws as much electricity as *a major metro*. The grid, not the chip, is starting to set the pace."
- On the 12% stat: add "high-end" or "base-case" qualifier. Or swap to the central-case number (~9–10%) and note the high case separately.
- Add the "as of" date to the ~5-yr interconnection wait stat (PJM 2025 queue analysis).
- Consider adding a fourth stat card: the $/MWh delta between AI-PPA prices and wholesale in ERCOT/PJM, which is the investable number.

## 6 · Fact-check addendum (2026-04-24)

Follow-up research on the five "claims that will get pushed back on" from the executive summary. Each claim was verified against public reporting via web search.

### 6.1 — xAI / SpaceX $1.25T merger (slide 10) — **TRUE**
The merger is real and widely reported. SpaceX acquired xAI via share exchange (0.1433 SpaceX share per xAI share) at a combined $1.25T valuation ($1T SpaceX + $250B xAI). Announced Feb 2, 2026 (the deck's date) per Bloomberg and CNBC. Stated rationale: orbital data centers. The combined entity is preparing for a ~$75B IPO later in 2026.

**Review update:** My original flag was wrong. The claim is accurate. The deck's citation is fine. *No change needed.*

### 6.2 — Anthropic ~13× ARR on $380B (slide 10) — **DEFENSIBLE BUT NEEDS A FOOTNOTE**
The $380B valuation is the Feb 12, 2026 Series G post-money (Anthropic's own press release). Anthropic's ARR trajectory (per Sacra and SaaStr):
- YE 2025: ~$9B
- Feb 2026 (Series G close): ~$14B
- March 2026: ~$19B
- April 2026: ~$30B

So $380B / ~$30B (April ARR) = ~12.7× → ~13× is correct *if* you pair the Feb 2026 valuation with April 2026 ARR. At the Series G close, the multiple was ~27×. Both are citable, but mixing them without disclosure is sloppy.

Also worth noting: by April 24, 2026, secondary-market valuations reportedly hit $1T (BigGo Finance). The $380B post-money is already stale.

**Review update:** My original flag ("implies ~$29B ARR, not supported by public reporting") was wrong — ARR has indeed grown to ~$30B by April 2026. But the slide should label the multiple as "~13× April 2026 ARR against the Feb 2026 post-money" so the dated mismatch is transparent. *Minor wording fix recommended, not a retraction.*

### 6.3 — "~48% hyperscaler net debt/EBITDA" (slide 14) — **METRIC IS MISLABELED**
The 48% figure is real but the metric name is wrong. Per CreditSights (Q3 2025), hyperscalers' **liabilities-to-assets ratio** fell to 48% (down from a 59% peak in late 2022). That is a balance-sheet ratio in percentage terms, not a cash-flow leverage multiple.

Actual net debt/EBITDA for the group is far lower and dispersed:
- Oracle: ~3.9–4.8× (noted as "junk-adjacent" in credit commentary)
- Microsoft / Alphabet / Meta: near net-cash or <1×
- Amazon: conservative per internal metrics

**The "~80% S&P" comparator is also a liabilities-to-assets ratio, not net debt/EBITDA.**

**Review update:** The 48% number is right; it's just labeled as the wrong metric. Fix: change the row label from "BUYER BALANCE SHEETS / ~48% hyperscaler net debt/EBITDA (vs ~80% S&P)" to "~48% hyperscaler **liabilities-to-assets** (vs ~80% S&P)." Or, to keep the net-debt-/-EBITDA framing, switch the number to "~1–2× avg ex-Oracle" (or whatever the aggregate figure is) and cite accordingly. *Label fix is required.*

### 6.4 — "Reasoning 2023–24" timeline (slide 4) — **DATE WRONG**
Confirmed from primary sources:
- OpenAI o1-preview: **Sept 12, 2024** (OpenAI blog, Bloomberg)
- OpenAI o1 full: **Dec 5, 2024** (VentureBeat)
- DeepSeek R1: **Jan 20, 2025** (DeepSeek API docs, Wikipedia, IISS)

So the Reasoning era begins in **late 2024** and accelerates through **2025**. Not 2023–24.

**Review update:** Change "2023–24" → "2024–25" on the Reasoning phase card. This is a clean factual fix. *Required.*

### 6.5 — "1 GW = mid-sized city" vs "~750K US homes" (slide 9) — **STAT IS RIGHT; ANALOGY IS WRONG**
1 GW continuous data-center load ≈ ~700K US homes (Congressional Research Service R48646) or ~1.8M people. The deck's 750K figure is slightly generous but within range.

However, 1.8M people is a **major metro** — Atlanta city + close-ring suburbs, Kansas City metro, or greater-Portland. A "mid-sized city" in common usage is Tulsa (~400K pop), Louisville (~630K), Wichita (~400K) — nothing near 1.8M.

**Review update:** The 750K-homes stat stands. Change the subhead from "as much electricity as a mid-sized city" to "as much electricity as a major metro" or "as much electricity as a city of nearly 2 million." *Subhead fix required.*
