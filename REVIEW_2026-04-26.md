## 2 · Executive summary

1. **Six slides still ship with literal `IMAGE:` art-direction placeholder text in the rendered slide body** (slides 12, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21). The dashed gray boxes are tolerable; the italicized prompt text inside them ("Screenshot — dark-mode IDE, AI agent editing Python code live, glowing orange cursor…") is not. P0 ship-blocker.
2. **Slide 11 contradicts itself on its own thesis.** The subhead claims Tech is growing "4× faster — at a *lower* multiple." The bar chart on the left agrees (Tech P/E 23× vs Non-Tech 27×). The dark payoff band directly underneath says "Tech median: ~26× fwd P/E … Non-Tech: ~23×" — Tech *higher*. Recomputing from the `TOP20` table in `build-deck.js` (Tech median P/E ≈ 25.5, Non-Tech median ≈ 22.7), the dark band is right and the bar chart's hard-coded `23 / 27` plus the "lower multiple" framing in the subhead are wrong. This is a P0; it's the headline-breaking error of the deck.
3. **Slide 23 is incomplete in the file as it sits on disk** (the XML stream stops mid-tag), the `.pptx` itself has no zip end-of-central-directory record, and the title "Appendix — sources (1 of 2)" implies a missing 2-of-2 slide. The deck cannot be opened by python-pptx or markitdown today. PowerPoint may be lenient enough to render it; nothing else will be. P0 — re-run `node build-deck.js` and confirm 24 slides cleanly write before circulating.
4. **Title quality is uneven.** Strong takeaway titles: 6, 9, 14 (rhetorical hook), 15, 21. Topic-label titles that force the subhead to do all the work: 4, 5, 7, 8, 10, 11, 12, 13, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 22. For an institutional roadshow deck every content title should be a full-sentence conclusion. Right now ~12 of 21 content titles are just labels with periods.
5. **Several hero claims will get challenged in the room without better sourcing.** "~48% hyperscaler net debt/EBITDA (vs ~80% S&P)" (slide 14) is suspicious — MSFT/GOOGL/META aggregate to net-cash today. The xAI–SpaceX "$1.25T all-stock merger closed Feb 2, 2026" (slide 10) and the implied Anthropic ARR (~$29B from $380B / 13×, slide 10) are both extraordinary and need a stronger citation than "Bloomberg/CNBC" / "Sacra/Bloomberg." "~12% of US electricity by 2028" (slide 9) is the top of the EPRI range, not a point estimate. Use `update-equity-returns` (or the verification path it documents) to spot-check before sending.
6. **Chart consistency breaks twice.** Slide 6 mixes comparison sets — adoption is AI vs Internet vs *PC*; revenue is AI vs *Cloud* vs Internet — and the chart objects exist (CHART[1], CHART[2]) but their underlying series are not visible from the parse, so I can't confirm whether the visual claims still match the headline. Slide 11's scatter plot quietly clips Tesla at 50× P/E (true 190×) and clips NVDA/AVGO growth at +50% (true 73% / 54%). The clipping is annotated in the source comment but not on the rendered chart.
7. **Iconographic risk.** Slides 13–18 use Unicode glyphs as icons (`⚙ ⚡ ↑ ◉ ✕ ↓ ▲ ▦ ☰ ◫ ▣ § ⚒ ▣`) at sizes up to 48 pt. These render fine in Calibri / Segoe UI but are font-dependent — on a presenter machine without those fonts, they will fall back to tofu boxes or differently-shaped substitutes. For a roadshow deck the safer move is SVG/PNG icon glyphs.
8. **Footer numbering and TOC pagination disagree.** TOC says TAKEAWAYS = "22"; the deck has 23 slides on disk. The appendix section is not represented in the TOC at all. Either add an "07 APPENDIX" row to slide 2 or remove the appendix from the file.

## 3 · Slide-by-slide review

### Slide 1 — AI Markets (cover)
**What works:** Confident, restrained cover. Yellow background, single-line wordmark at 88 pt Arial Black, short orange accent rule above it, plain "May 2026" date. Black footer disclaimer is in place.
**What doesn't:** Nothing prevents this from being mistaken for a generic market-update template — it does not say "Strategy Research" or any author/firm anywhere. The disclaimer footer ("FOR INFORMATIONAL PURPOSES ONLY…") sits flush on every slide *including* the cover, which is unusual; covers usually suppress the legal strip.
**Specific suggestions:**
- Add an author/firm wordmark to the bottom-left of SP[5], e.g. small "Strategy Research · May 2026" in 10 pt above the legal strip.
- Suppress the black footer bar (SP[4]) on the cover only — the strip should start on slide 2.
- Optional: tighten the orange rule (SP[1]) so its right edge aligns flush with the 'A' of "AI Markets" rather than floating 0.1" early.

### Slide 2 — Contents.
**What works:** Clean six-row TOC; the 0.12"-wide colored sidebar tiles do real work tying section colors (teal/gold/orange/red/purple/black) to their downstream slides; the right-rail page numbers are aligned and read cleanly.
**What doesn't:** (a) Title is "Contents." with a trailing period — institutional decks default to "Contents" or "Table of contents" without one; the period reads twee. (b) The footer marks this slide "2" but the TOC's last entry, "TAKEAWAYS · 22", is followed by 1+ appendix slides not represented in the TOC at all. (c) Single-word section labels ("LANDSCAPE", "MARKET", "SHIFTS", "RISKS", "FRONTIER", "TAKEAWAYS") are all-caps and identical-weight — the eye has nothing to anchor on. (d) Page-range "12 – 13" for SHIFTS only covers 2 slides; "22" for TAKEAWAYS is one slide; the section weights are visibly imbalanced.
**Specific suggestions:**
- Drop the period from "Contents."
- Add row 07 "APPENDIX · 23 – 24" once the appendix is rebuilt — or, if you keep appendix off-TOC, label slide 23+ with their own footer style ("A1 / A2") so the audience knows they're reference material.
- Tighten SHIFTS (slides 12–13) — two slides for a section between MARKET (5 slides) and RISKS (4 slides) feels under-developed; either merge it back into MARKET or split off another SHIFTS slide so the section has at least three.
- Optional: italicize the page numbers in 12 pt regular weight to break up the all-caps wall.

### Slide 3 — Why AI matters to markets.
**What works:** Three big numbered tiles (orange/teal/gold) with a one-line headline and a one-line backing claim each — exactly the right density for a section-opener. Source line cites CreditSights / MUFG and Jensen Huang. Title is one of the better ones in the deck.
**What doesn't:** The capex figure ("Big 5 hyperscalers are pouring roughly $750B into AI infrastructure in 2026 alone — more than the entire US energy buildout at its peak") is the single most consequential number in the deck and it sits on slide 3 as a plain-text claim with no chart. The "more than the entire US energy buildout at its peak" comparison is also unsourced and will provoke pushback (which buildout? what year? what definition of "energy buildout"?).
**Specific suggestions:**
- Replace the "more than the entire US energy buildout at its peak" simile with a concrete dated reference (e.g. "vs. ~$240B for the 1996–2001 telecom buildout (Yardeni/BEA)") or remove it.
- Consider promoting the "$750B in 2026 alone" stat into a 60-pt callout in the right margin of tile 01 — give it the visual weight its centrality deserves.
- Bullet 02 ("It touches every layer of the economy") is the weakest of the three — vague and harder to defend than the other two. Replace with a specific share-of-S&P claim ("AI-linked names = ~30% of US large-cap free float") to match the slide-22 takeaway.

### Slide 4 — AI evolution from chatbots to agents.
**What works:** Six-phase color-coded timeline reads cleanly left-to-right. The accent-colored side bar inside each card is a nice device. Subhead does the heavy lifting: "Software that learns from data, reasons through problems, and — increasingly — takes action without human direction."
**What doesn't:** (a) Title is misleading — the timeline starts in the 1950s with Symbolic AI, not with chatbots. "From chatbots to agents" undersells the slide. (b) Date ranges overlap and confuse: "Reasoning · 2023–24" and "LLM chatbots · 2022–24" are both 2022–24, with one labeled 2023 and the other 2022. (c) Source line says only "Strategy Research" — a six-phase historical timeline that will be challenged on its dates needs at least one external scholar/textbook reference. (d) Each card is 1.18" × 2.55" tall but the text reaches only 30–40% of the way down — the bottom 60% of each card is empty gray space.
**Specific suggestions:**
- Retitle: "AI's six waves — from rules to autonomous agents." or "Sixty years of AI in six steps — and we are now in step six."
- Reconcile dates: LLM chatbots = 2022–24 (ChatGPT Nov 2022); Reasoning = 2024–25 (o1 Sep 2024, R1 Jan 2025); Agentic = 2025+. Today's "Reasoning · 2023–24" predates o1.
- Add a citation to the timeline (Stuart Russell *AI: A Modern Approach* 4e, or AI Index 2025) under the source line.
- Either tighten card height to 1.5" or add a one-line "key system" example bullet inside each card to fill the empty bottom (e.g. "AlphaGo, ImageNet" inside Deep Learning).

### Slide 5 — The AI stack.
**What works:** Clean four-section grouping (User · Compute · Silicon · Materials) using accent bars; readable on a single look; orientation note at the bottom ("top closest to user, bottom closest to atoms") is useful.
**What doesn't:** (a) Title is a topic label. (b) Eleven rows × 0.35" each = 3.85" — the rows are uniform-height and visually indistinguishable; the eye reads it as a wall, not a hierarchy. (c) The four-section grouping (USER / COMPUTE / SILICON / MATERIALS) is rendered as 0.28"-wide colored sidebars on the left only — without a horizontal divider line between groups they bleed into each other visually. (d) Right-side descriptions are all 10.5 pt and identical weight; nothing pops.
**Specific suggestions:**
- Retitle: "Eleven layers, four power blocs — your AI exposure depends on which layer you own."
- Add a thin 1-pt horizontal divider between groups (between "Networking"/"Packaging", between "Foundry"/"Equipment", between "Equipment"/"Raw materials") so the four blocs read as four blocs.
- Bold the layer names on the right-side description column at 11 pt so the eye walks down the spine of layer names, not the descriptions.
- Optional: add a small column on the far right with one or two ticker leaders per layer (e.g. "Models → OPEN, ANTH (priv); Foundry → TSM; Equipment → ASML, AMAT, LRCX, KLAC") — this is the slide an audience will photograph, and right now it has no investable hooks.

### Slide 6 — AI growth rates are unprecedented.
**What works:** Strong takeaway title that survives without the subhead. Bottom payoff band is well-placed and reinforces the headline.
**What doesn't:** (a) Comparison sets aren't parallel: left chart is AI vs Internet vs *PC*; right chart is AI vs *Cloud* vs Internet — the audience will ask "where's PC on the right? where's Cloud on the left?" (b) The legend strip "AI · OpenAI · Anthropic | CLOUD · AWS · Azure · Google Cloud | INTERNET · Meta · Google" assigns concrete companies to each label — but the source line says "Cloud and Internet trajectories are illustrative", which contradicts the apparent precision of the legend. (c) Charts are referenced (CHART[1], CHART[2]) but their series data is not in the parse — I cannot independently verify the AI line's right-end value, the chart axis, the % of US population denominator, or the units consistency between left ("% of US population") and right ("Revenue $B"). (d) Source mixes Mary Meeker (2019) — six years old — with "consensus estimates" without attribution.
**Specific suggestions:**
- Pick one comparison set across both panels: AI vs Internet vs Cloud (drop PC on the left), or AI vs Internet vs PC (drop Cloud on the right). Mixed sets confuse.
- If the lines on the left are illustrative, label them in-chart as "illustrative" and remove the company strip ("AI · OpenAI · Anthropic …") which suggests precision.
- Replace Mary Meeker 2019 with a more current trace (e.g. Pew internet adoption data + Statista PC adoption); 2019 vintage on a 2026 deck reads stale.
- Confirm independently that the "100M users in 2 months" ChatGPT line maps to the % adoption denominator used here — that figure is global, not US.

### Slide 7 — Semiconductors, explained.
**What works:** The two-tier layout (four process stages on top, six product categories below) is genuinely clarifying. The yellow payoff band ("Logic and memory absorb the AI capex — but every chip flows through ASML tools, TSMC fabs, and advanced packaging…") is the takeaway the title should be.
**What doesn't:** (a) Title — "Semiconductors, explained" — is condescending in this room (institutional investors don't need an explainer flagged as such) and doesn't tell them what they'll learn. (b) The right-pointing chevrons (›) between stages are 24 pt and slightly off-baseline against the 11 pt stage labels. (c) "DESIGN" → "EQUIPMENT" → "FOUNDRY" → "PACKAGING" is a logical-flow ordering, but the actual physical flow is design → equipment makes tools → tools go to foundry → packaging — having Equipment between Design and Foundry confuses readers who expect strictly serial flow. (d) Six product cards have identical visual weight; LOGIC and MEMORY (the two cards the takeaway is about) should be highlighted to match the headline argument.
**Specific suggestions:**
- Retitle to the existing yellow-banner takeaway: "Logic and memory absorb the capex — but ASML, TSMC, and advanced packaging decide who ships."
- Rework the stages row: keep four stages but show "DESIGN → FOUNDRY (using equipment) → PACKAGING" with EQUIPMENT below the arrow as a feeder tier, rather than inline.
- Visually elevate LOGIC (orange header bar) and MEMORY (gold header bar) — already accent-colored; consider a thicker 2-pt border or a 4% darker fill to push them forward of ANALOG/SENSORS/DISCRETE.
- Citation list ("SIA / WSTS / SEMI / TrendForce / company disclosures") is appropriately broad — keep.

### Slide 8 — Who's buying the chips.
**What works:** The four-buyer card grid below is well-structured, with consistent card heights, top color-coded headers, and a "TOP BUYERS" footer in each card.
**What doesn't:** (a) Title is a topic label — the takeaway "Five buyers fund most of the AI compute cycle — their capex IS the market" sitting in the yellow payoff band is what the title should be. (b) The stacked bar at the top has segments sized 5.22" / 1.08" / 0.9" / 1.8" (= 58% / 12% / 10% / 20%). The 0.9"-wide ENTERPRISE segment cannot fit "ENTERPRISE" at 10 pt without wrapping or clipping; SOVEREIGNS at 1.08" is on the edge. Verify visually — these are likely wrapping mid-word ("ENTERPR/ISE", "SOVEREIGN/S"). (c) The bar above is the only quantitative element; the four cards below are all qualitative. The slide's claim ("five hyperscalers fund roughly 60%") is in text only, not in any chart label. (d) "Estimated share of advanced-AI chip demand, 2026E" is shown as a thin label between the bar and the cards — but the cards do not show the actual % share for each buyer; the audience has to map back to bar widths to recover the number.
**Specific suggestions:**
- Retitle: "Five hyperscalers fund roughly 60% of advanced-AI chip demand — their capex IS the market."
- Rework the bar so segment sizes carry inline percentage labels above each segment ("58% · 12% · 10% · 20%"), not just colors. Move the ENTERPRISE/SOVEREIGNS labels *above* each segment so the wrap pressure is gone.
- Add a "% share" stat to the top-right of each of the four cards (orange-fill chip "12%" inside SOVEREIGNS card, etc.) so the cards carry the numerical claim, not the bar alone.
- Sources line is solid (SemiAnalysis · Omdia · TrendForce · hyperscaler 2026 capex guides · IDC). Keep — and add an "(estimates 2026E)" tag inline so the directional nature is unmistakable.

### Slide 9 — Power is the new bottleneck.
**What works:** Title is one of the strongest in the deck — full-sentence takeaway. Three hero stats (~12% / 1 GW / ~5 yr) at 40 pt are the visual anchor and they communicate the slide's argument before the reader gets to the cards.
**What doesn't:** (a) "~12% of US electricity demand will go to data centers by 2028" — EPRI's 2024 report puts this at 4.6%–9.1% by 2030 in the central case; 12% is the *high* end of their range, and the slide presents it as a single point estimate. (b) "1 GW typical new AI campus" is the high end (Stargate Phase 1 is 1.2 GW, but the median new hyperscale build is ~150–300 MW). The "~750K US homes" comparison reads like a headline but the math is ~830 W/home × 8,760 h ≈ 7,300 kWh/yr/home, which fits — keep the comparison, soften the "typical." (c) "Avg 5 yr wait for new grid interconnection in PJM & ERCOT" elides the fact that most of those queues are renewables, not data-center-specific connections; the figure exists but the framing is loose. (d) The three power-source columns are logically ordered (Gas / Nuclear / Renewables) but the cards are nearly empty below the 100-word body text — large gray zones.
**Specific suggestions:**
- Reword "~12%" to "up to ~12%" or "EPRI's high case" inline, or replace with the central ~9% by 2030 estimate.
- Replace "1 GW typical new AI campus" with "1 GW for the largest new AI campuses (~750K US homes)" — qualifying "largest" — to inoculate against the "median is 250 MW" rebuttal.
- Replace "PJM & ERCOT" 5-year claim with a more specific tag: "5-year wait, PJM new-load queue, 2025."
- Tighten the GAS/NUCLEAR/RENEWABLES card heights to ~1.4" (currently 1.55") and add one extra stat under the body text — e.g. "Backlog: $XB" or "PPA price: $X/MWh" — to fill the empty space and reinforce the investability.
- Citation list (EPRI · DOE · LBNL · MSFT/Constellation TMI · PJM/ERCOT) is excellent; keep as-is.

### Slide 10 — The labs.
**What works:** Four-row table compresses a lot of dimensional information (valuation, multiple, recent event) into a tight grid; image thumbnails on the left tie row to identity. Citation discipline is high — five distinct numbered footnotes for five distinct claims.
**What doesn't:** (a) Title is a topic label. (b) The xAI/SpaceX "$1.25T all-stock merger closed Feb 2, 2026" claim is extraordinary and deserves more than one footnote. The implied $1.25T is "combined" enterprise value, but presented in the same column ($852B / $380B / $2.3T / $1.25T) where the other three rows are firm-only valuations — apples-to-oranges. (d) The 14 pt "Q1 2026 funding to foundational AI startups was 2× all of 2025" payoff line at the bottom is a great stat but it sits under the table without visual emphasis, and the "2×" comparison year (2025) is itself a uniquely big year — the multiplier reads stronger than it is.
**Specific suggestions:**

- Show Anthropic's implied ARR explicitly inside the row: "$380B · ~13× ARR · ~$29B implied ARR · $30B Series G (Feb)". If the implied ARR isn't $29B, the multiple is wrong; if it is, say so.
- For the xAI/SpaceX row, label the $1.25T as "combined EV" and show the pre-merger xAI valuation in a smaller secondary line ("xAI standalone: $X B as of Dec 2025") so the audience can compare like-to-like.
- Add a 7-pt "as of" date under the four-row table to make clear all valuations are pinned to the same instant.
- Verify the merger closing date and source — "TechCrunch / CNBC" is a press citation, not a primary one. If the deck cannot point to an SEC filing or a 13D for an event of this magnitude, downgrade the claim.

### Slide 11 — Earnings & valuations.
**What works:** Best-engineered slide in the deck. The TOP20 table is hand-built with explicit confidence levels, the medians are computed, and the right-side scatter plot is genuinely informative — it positions every name on the price-vs-growth plane.
**What doesn't:** **The slide contradicts itself on its own thesis.** The subhead says "Top 10 Tech firms are growing 4× faster than the top 10 Non-Tech — at a *lower* multiple." The bar chart on the left agrees: Tech P/E 23×, Non-Tech P/E 27×. But the dark payoff band immediately below the bar chart says: "Tech median: ~26× fwd P/E on ~17% forward revenue growth. Non-Tech: ~23× on ~5%." Recomputing from the TOP20 table in `build-deck.js`: Tech median P/E = 25.6 (between MSFT 25.6 and TSM 26.5), Non-Tech median P/E = 22.65 (between BRK.B 21.6 and V 23.7). **The dark band is right; the bar chart's hard-coded "23 × / 27×" and the "lower multiple" framing in the subhead are both wrong.**
Other issues: (b) the bar chart shows "23x" with a lowercase x and 10.5 pt — the dark band shows "26×" with the proper Unicode multiplication sign — typographic inconsistency. (c) The right-side scatter clips Tesla at 50× P/E (true 190×), NVDA growth at +50% (true 73%), AVGO growth at +50% (true 54%) — the clip is annotated in the source comment of build-deck.js but is *not* labeled on the chart itself, which is a credibility hit when a sharp viewer recognizes Tesla on the X axis at the wrong P/E. (d) The slide has fourteen ticker labels on the scatter and several overlap (Microsoft / Alphabet, Apple / Amazon).
**Specific suggestions:**
- **Fix the contradiction first.** Update the bar chart's `metrics` array in `build-deck.js` to read 26× (Tech) / 23× (Non-Tech), AND change the subhead to "Top 10 Tech firms are growing 4× faster than the top 10 Non-Tech — at a *modestly higher* multiple" or "at a *similar* multiple." The "lower multiple" framing as currently stated is empirically false on the deck's own data.
- On the scatter, add inline annotations for clipped points: "(NVDA: 73% — clipped)", "(AVGO: 54% — clipped)", "(TSLA: 190× — clipped)". A sophisticated reader will catch the clip; pre-empt it.
- Standardize the ×: use "26×" everywhere (Unicode U+00D7), not "26x" or "23x".
- Repel overlapping labels (Microsoft/Alphabet/Apple/Amazon are clustering around 6.9, 3.3); offset Microsoft and Alphabet by ±0.05" vertically. This is mostly mechanical.
- Operating margin (41% Tech / 20% Non-Tech) is presented in the bars with no source — the TOP20 table doesn't carry margin data. Add "Op margin: company filings 2025 TTM" to the source line.

### Slide 13 — The divergence.
